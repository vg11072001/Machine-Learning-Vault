Ridge & Lasso Glossary

Precise vocabulary adhered to across all lessons and expected in senior ML interviews. Print this page as a quick refresher.

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)
Linear regression estimator that minimizes Residual Sum of Squares: RSS(β) = ‖y − Xβ‖₂². Unbiased under Gauss–Markov assumptions, with closed form β̂ = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy when XᵀX is invertible, but high-variance when predictors are correlated or p ≈ n.
Bias–Variance Tradeoff
Decomposition of expected test MSE into Bias² + Variance + Irreducible Noise. Regularization deliberately introduces a small bias by shrinking coefficients toward zero in exchange for a much larger drop in estimator variance, lowering total test MSE.
Multicollinearity (Nonorthogonality)
High linear correlation among predictor columns of X. Causes the smallest eigenvalues of XᵀX to approach zero, inflating Var(β̂) = σ²(XᵀX)⁻¹ so correlated coefficients explode with huge opposite signs.
Condition Number κ(XᵀX)
Ratio of largest to smallest eigenvalue of XᵀX: κ = d₁² / dₚ². Large κ means tiny noise in y causes massive swings in β̂. Ridge lowers it to (d₁² + λ) / (dₚ² + λ).
L₂ Penalty / Ridge Regularization (Tikhonov Regularization)
Adds λ Σ βⱼ² = λ‖β‖₂² (excluding intercept β₀) to RSS. Strictly convex, differentiable everywhere, yields closed-form (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀy, shrinks correlated weights smoothly together, never produces exact zeros for finite λ.
L₁ Penalty / Lasso (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator)
Adds λ Σ |βⱼ| = λ‖β‖₁ (excluding intercept β₀) to RSS. Convex but non-differentiable at βⱼ = 0. Geometrically constrains β inside an L₁ cross-polytope (diamond in 2D) whose sharp axis corners produce sparse solutions (exact zeros).
Feature Standardization (Z-scoring)
Transforming each predictor to mean 0 and variance 1: x̃ⱼ = (xⱼ − μⱼ) / sⱼ. Mandatory before Ridge/Lasso/Elastic Net because penalties depend on raw coefficient magnitude; unscaled features with small numeric units have artificially huge coefficients and get unfairly crushed. Always fit μⱼ, sⱼ on training folds only.
Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)
Factorization X = U D Vᵀ where U, V are orthonormal and D = diag(d₁, …, dₚ) holds singular values. Reveals that Ridge shrinks the j-th principal component coordinate of y by dⱼ² / (dⱼ² + λ)—penalizing low-variance directions most heavily.
Effective Degrees of Freedom df(λ)
Measure of model complexity for Ridge: df(λ) = tr(X(XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀ) = Σ dⱼ² / (dⱼ² + λ). Equals p at λ = 0 (OLS) and approaches 0 as λ → ∞. For Lasso, unbiased degrees of freedom equals the expected number of active (non-zero) predictors.
Subgradient & Subdifferential
Generalization of the derivative for convex, non-smooth functions like |β|. At β = 0, the subdifferential of |β| is the entire interval [−1, +1]. A point is optimal when 0 belongs to the subdifferential of the objective.
Soft-Thresholding Operator S(z, γ)
Closed-form minimizer of ½(β − z)² + γ|β|: equals z − γ if z > γ, z + γ if z < −γ, and 0 if |z| ≤ γ. Drives Lasso's exact solution in orthonormal designs and each inner step of Coordinate Descent.
Cyclical Coordinate Descent
Fast iterative solver (used by glmnet and scikit-learn) that minimizes the Lasso or Elastic Net objective one coordinate βⱼ at a time via closed-form soft-thresholding while holding other weights fixed. Converges to the global minimum because the non-smooth penalty is separable across coordinates.
LARS (Least Angle Regression)
Piecewise-linear path algorithm (Efron et al., 2004) that computes the entire Lasso regularization path across all λ values in roughly the cost of a single OLS fit (with the Lasso modification dropping variables when they cross zero).
Elastic Net
Convex combination of L₁ and L₂ penalties: λ[α‖β‖₁ + ½(1−α)‖β‖₂²] (Zou & Hastie, 2005). Keeps Lasso's sparsity while inheriting Ridge's strict convexity, enabling the grouping effect on correlated features and removing Lasso's min(n, p) cap on selected features.
MAP Equivalence (Gaussian vs. Laplace Prior)
Bayesian interpretation of regularization: minimizing RSS + λ‖β‖₂² equals Maximum A Posteriori estimation under a zero-mean Gaussian prior on β; minimizing RSS + λ‖β‖₁ equals MAP under a zero-mean Laplace (double-exponential) prior.
One-Standard-Error (1-SE) Rule
Cross-validation heuristic from Hastie et al.: instead of choosing the λ with the absolute lowest CV error, choose the largest λ (simplest, most regularized model) whose CV error is within one standard error of the minimum. Favors parsimony and guards against CV overfitting.
Post-Selection Inference & Relaxed Lasso
Standard OLS p-values and confidence intervals are invalid if you run Lasso to select variables and then refit OLS on the same data (data snooping inflates false positives). Relaxed Lasso (or sample splitting) decouples variable selection from coefficient estimation to reduce L₁ shrinkage bias on strong signals.
