Bayesian Priors, Elastic Net, Cross-Validation & Production Traps

This is where good candidates become "strong hire": probabilistic MAP proofs, Elastic Net's grouping theorem, CV leakage traps, post-selection inference, and the bridge to AdamW in deep learning.

~10 min read + Bayesian prior lab Win: Master every advanced follow-up interviewers love

1. The Bayesian View: Ridge & Lasso Are MAP Estimators

Suppose targets follow a Gaussian noise model y | X, β ~ N(Xβ, σ²I), and we place a prior p(β) on the coefficients. By Bayes' rule, finding the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) mode maximizes log p(y | X, β) + log p(β), which is equivalent to minimizing the negative log posterior:[Tibshirani 1996]

  1. Gaussian prior βⱼ ~ N(0, τ²) ⇒ Ridge (L₂):
    −log p(y|X,β) − log p(β) = (1 / 2σ²)‖y − Xβ‖₂² + (1 / 2τ²) Σ βⱼ² + const Why this connects λ to beliefs: Multiplying by 2σ² recovers Ridge with λ = σ² / τ². Stronger belief that weights are small (smaller prior variance τ²) OR noisier data (larger σ²) means a larger λ!
  2. Laplace (Double-Exponential) prior βⱼ ~ Laplace(0, b) ⇒ Lasso (L₁):
    p(βⱼ) = (1 / 2b) exp(−|βⱼ| / b)  ⇒  −log p(β) = (1 / b) Σ |βⱼ| + const Why this yields sparsity & heavier tails: Multiplying by 2σ² recovers Lasso with λ = 2σ² / b. Compared to a Gaussian of equal variance, a Laplace prior has a sharp cusp at 0 (concentrating mass at exact zero) and heavier tails (less harsh on occasional huge true effects).

Subtle Senior Caveat

Lasso equals the MAP mode of a Laplace posterior, not the posterior mean! A full Bayesian Lasso (averaging over the posterior) actually yields dense, non-zero posterior means — it is taking the mode at the cusp that produces exact zeros.

2. Elastic Net: Fixing Both of Lasso's Failure Modes

Zou & Hastie (2005) combined L₁ and L₂ into the Elastic Net objective:[Zou & Hastie 2005]

JEN(β) = (1 / 2n)‖y − Xβ‖₂² + λ [ α‖β‖₁ + ½(1 − α)‖β‖₂² ] In scikit-learn: alpha is overall λ, and l1_ratio is the mixing weight α ∈ [0, 1] (0 = Ridge, 1 = Lasso).[sklearn §1.1.5]
  • How it solves correlated groups ("Grouping Effect"): The L₂ term makes the objective strictly convex for any α < 1, curving the sides of the L₁ diamond outward. Two strongly correlated predictors now enter or exit the model together with nearly equal weights instead of Lasso flipping a coin between them.
  • How it breaks Lasso's p > n saturation cap: Algebraically, adding λ₂‖β‖₂² is equivalent to appending p pseudo-rows √λ₂ · Iₚ (with target 0) to X! That boosts the augmented design matrix to full column rank p, allowing Elastic Net to select more than n predictors even when p ≫ n (crucial in genomics or text TF-IDF).
  • Trade-off: You must tune two hyperparameters (λ and l1_ratio) on a 2D CV grid, costing more compute.

3. Tuning λ, Cross-Validation & Four Real-World Traps

Expand each production & interview trap

Trap 1: Data Leakage from Standardizing Before Cross-Validation

Wrong: X_scaled = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X) followed by cross_val_score(LassoCV(), X_scaled, y).

Right: Wrap scaler + estimator inside a Pipeline([('scaler', StandardScaler()), ('model', LassoCV())]) so mean and standard deviation are computed strictly on each training split.

Why: Fitting the scaler on the entire dataset leaks validation fold distribution statistics into training folds — especially dangerous in small-sample or time-series splits.

Trap 2: Choosing λ_min vs. the 1-SE Rule

How: Plot CV MSE across a log-spaced grid of λ (e.g., 10⁻⁴ to 10⁴). Compute both the mean CV error and its standard error across folds.

Why use the 1-SE rule: CV error curves are often flat near the minimum. Picking the absolute minimum λ_min can overfit the CV folds and retain noisy features. The 1-SE rule picks the largest λ (simplest, most regularized model) whose CV error is within 1 standard error of λ_min.[ESL §7.10]

Trap 3: Invalid p-values After Lasso ("Post-Selection Inference")

The bug: Running Lasso to select 10 features, then fitting OLS on those 10 features on the same data and reporting OLS p-values or confidence intervals.

Why it is invalid: You used y twice — once to cherry-pick features that happened to correlate strongly with y in this sample, and again to test whether they correlate with y! That inflates false positives massively.

How to fix it: Use sample splitting (select features on split A, fit OLS & compute p-values on split B), or formal selective-inference methods. Note: if you only want lower prediction bias (not p-values), Relaxed Lasso (tuning a blend between Lasso and post-Lasso OLS via CV) is valid and often improves test MSE.

Trap 4: L₂ Regularization vs. Weight Decay in Deep Learning (SGD vs. AdamW)

Why interviewers ask this for ML Engineer roles: Under vanilla SGD, adding ½λ‖w‖₂² to the loss adds λw to the gradient, so the weight update is w ← w − η(∇L + λw) = (1 − ηλ)w − η∇L — identical to weight decay.

Where they diverge: In adaptive optimizers like Adam, adding L₂ to the loss sends λw through the moving-average variance denominator √vₜ, so weights with large historical gradients get less regularization! AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) decouples weight decay so w decays directly by (1 − ηλ)w outside the adaptive step.

Bonus: Logistic Regression & GLMs with L₁ / L₂ (The C = 1/λ Convention)

Regularization extends directly to classification by replacing RSS with negative log-likelihood (cross-entropy): min −log L(β) + λ · Penalty(β). In scikit-learn's LogisticRegression, the parameter is C = 1 / λ (inverse regularization strength): smaller C means stronger regularization! Watch out for this classic coding-interview flip.
